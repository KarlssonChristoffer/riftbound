Style B · Three.js, rendered small

Pixelated 3D

A real 3D scene rendered at a few hundred pixels wide, then scaled up sharp. Four tricks make it read as pixel art:

  • Pixel textures. Floors and walls use the sprite mode's pixel art, sized so one texel lands on one screen pixel.
  • Smooth light on top. Light is rendered as its own layer and blended over the pixel art with soft edges. Torch pools, spell glows and shadows stay smooth while everything else stays crisp.
  • Outlines and highlights. Dark lines come from the depth buffer and light creases from the surface normals.
  • Pixel-snapped camera. It moves in whole pixels and slides the final image by the leftover fraction, so following the hero never shimmers.
Gains
Keeps your models, rigs, spells and camera. It's a render pipeline bolted on, not a rewrite.
Costs

The sprite sheet

Everything the sprite mode draws, at 4×. I write the left half of each figure and it gets mirrored. Letters swap to their shade on the way across, so the right side reads as the dark side.


      
This is the myconid as I wrote it. Each letter is a palette slot: o outline, C cap, w spots, s stem, e eyes. Mirrored, C becomes c (shaded cap) and s becomes S. Changing a palette recolors a monster for a new biome.
